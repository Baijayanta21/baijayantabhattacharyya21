About Me

I am a PhD student in the Department of Physics at IIT Kharagpur, advised by Prof. Somnath Bharadwaj. My doctoral research is centred on theoretical cosmology and astrophysics, with a particular emphasis on 21-cm intensity mapping as a probe of the early Universe.

Before joining IIT Kharagpur, I completed my Bachelor's and Master's degrees in Physics, building a foundation in general relativity, quantum field theory, and statistical mechanics. I am drawn to the large-scale structure of the cosmos and the physical processes that shaped the epoch of reionization.

I am also actively involved in developing data analysis pipelines for radio interferometric observations, working with both simulations and real data from the Murchison Widefield Array (MWA).

Current & Recent Work

Projects

z = 8.2 · MWA

Tracking Tapered Gridded Estimator for the 21-cm Power Spectrum

Development and application of a novel estimator to extract the 21-cm power spectrum from MWA drift-scan observations. This work delivered improved upper limits at z = 8.2 and was published in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society (2026).

Simulation

Simulations of the Epoch of Reionization

Running and analysing large-scale hydrodynamic and radiative transfer simulations to model the intergalactic medium during reionization, compared directly against 21-cm power spectrum predictions.

Pipeline · Python

Data Analysis Pipeline for Radio Interferometers

Building a modular pipeline for calibration, imaging, and power spectrum estimation using data from the MWA, designed to extend to upcoming SKA-pathfinder arrays.
