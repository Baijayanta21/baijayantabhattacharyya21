About Me

I am a PhD student in the Department of Physics at IIT Kharagpur, where I am advised by Prof. Somnath Bharadwaj. My doctoral research is centred on theoretical cosmology and astrophysics, with a particular emphasis on 21‑cm intensity mapping as a probe of the early Universe.

Before joining IIT Kharagpur, I completed my Bachelor's and Master's degrees in Physics, with a strong foundation in general relativity, quantum field theory, and statistical mechanics. I am deeply interested in the large‑scale structure of the cosmos and the physical processes that shaped the epoch of reionization.

I am also actively involved in the development of data analysis pipelines for radio interferometric observations, working with simulations and real data from the Murchison Widefield Array (MWA).

Education: PhD (ongoing) · IIT Kharagpur  |  MSc Physics  |  BSc Physics

Research interests: 21‑cm cosmology, reionization, dark matter, galaxy formation, radio interferometry.

Projects

Tracking Tapered Gridded Estimator for 21‑cm Power Spectrum

Development and application of a novel estimator to extract the 21‑cm power spectrum from MWA drift‑scan observations. This work led to improved upper limits at z = 8.2 and was published in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society (2026).

Simulations of the Epoch of Reionization

Running and analysing large‑scale hydrodynamic and radiative transfer simulations to model the intergalactic medium during reionization. Comparison with 21‑cm power spectrum predictions.

Data Analysis Pipeline for Radio Interferometers

Building a modular pipeline in Python for calibration, imaging, and power spectrum estimation using data from the MWA and upcoming SKA‑pathfinder arrays.
